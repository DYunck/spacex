How to use SPCX Signals

A 10-minute walkthrough: what each part of the dashboard means, a simple daily routine. The app never sends messages; everything is on the dashboard.

Read this firstThis app gives you information, not instructions. It never buys or sells anything. Its signals come from past price patterns, and in testing so far they've been right only about half the time. Options can lose 100% of what you pay. Every decision is yours.

1. Options in two minutes

An option is a contract that gives you the right to buy or sell 100 shares of SPCX at a set price (the strike) until a set date (the expiration).

  • Call: profits if SPCX goes up past the strike.
  • Put: profits if SPCX goes down below the strike.
  • Prices are quoted per share, so multiply by 100. An option quoted at $3.25 costs $325 per contract.
  • If you buy an option, the most you can lose is what you paid.

Three things decide whether an option makes money. Being right about the direction is only one of them:

  1. Direction: did the stock move the way you bet?
  2. Timing: did it move before time ran out? Options lose a little value every day (time decay), and the loss speeds up in the final three weeks.
  3. Price paid: were options cheap or expensive when you bought? If you buy when options are pricey, you can be right about direction and still lose money.

The dashboard is built around these three questions.

2. Tour of the dashboard

Open the dashboard and follow along from top to bottom.

The top bar

Shows SPCX's latest price, today's change and when the data was last updated. The 📅 Weekly recap link opens a page with one summary card per week (see below). Tap any small ⓘ next to a heading or number for a one-sentence explanation of it. Updates run every hour while the market is open, and prices can be about 15 minutes behind. A yellow banner appears if the data is more than a day old.

"Today in one paragraph"

The quickest read. It's written automatically from the numbers each hour: where SPCX is, the trend, the score with its main caution, whether options are cheap or expensive, how big a move options expect, and what event is coming next. On a busy day, this plus the main box is enough.

"At a glance"

Colored tags sum up direction, option prices and the next major event. Below them:

  • Daily score / hourly score: if these point in opposite directions, the signal is weaker.
  • Expected move: how far the options market thinks SPCX could move by a given date. For example, ±$7.88 (±4.8%) means option prices assume roughly a 4.8% swing either way. To profit from buying an option, the stock usually needs to move more than this.

The chart

Switch between Daily (one candle per day since the IPO) and Hourly (the last 30 trading days). Each candle is green if the price rose during that period and red if it fell.

  • Blue line: 20-period average, the short-term trend. Price above it is a good sign; below it is a weak one.
  • Orange line: 50-period average, the longer-term trend. Blue above orange means an uptrend.
  • Gray dotted lines (Bollinger bands): the normal price range. When price pushes outside them it's stretched and often snaps back.
  • Dashed horizontal lines: support (where buyers stepped in before) and resistance (where sellers did). The blue "IPO" line marks $135, a level traders watch.
  • Bars at the bottom: trading volume. Big moves on big volume matter more.
  • Purple line below the chart (RSI): above 70 is overbought and a pullback is more likely; below 30 is oversold and a bounce is more likely.

"Why — signals behind the score"

Every point in the score, in plain English and sorted by importance. Green numbers push toward bullish, red toward bearish. Read this before trusting the headline. If the biggest reasons are all hourly, the signal is short-term. If daily and hourly disagree, be cautious.

"Daily vs. hourly"

The same indicators side by side for both timeframes. The two lines worth checking most:

  • Volume vs. normal: 4.7× means more than four times the usual trading, so something is happening.
  • Actual volatility: how much SPCX has really been moving, which you'll compare with option prices in the next box.

"Are options cheap or expensive?"

This box is often more useful than the direction score.

  • Implied volatility (IV): how much movement option prices assume, as a yearly percentage.
  • Actual volatility: how much the stock really moved over the last 20 days.
  • Implied ÷ actual: above 1.3× means you're overpaying for options. Below 0.9× means options are relatively cheap.
  • IV rank: where today's IV sits compared with SPCX's own history (0 is the cheapest it's been, 100 the most expensive). It needs 20 trading days of collected data before it appears.
Volatility crushOption prices swell before earnings because everyone expects a big move. Right after the announcement they often collapse, even when the stock moves the way you bet. When the box warns about an event within a few days, buying options to hold through it is risky.

"Contracts worth a look"

Up to three specific options for calls and three for puts. Use the tabs to switch. They're chosen to be easy to trade (small gap between buy and sell prices, plenty of open contracts) and about 45% likely to finish profitable (a delta near 0.45). Columns:

"What if" payoff calculator

Shows roughly what an option would be worth at different SPCX prices and dates, so you can see the risk before you buy.

  1. Choose Call or Put, the expiration and the strike. The price fills in automatically; change it if your broker shows something different.
  2. Move the "Value on" slider to see how the option loses value as time passes. Slide it to the end to see the value at expiration.
  3. Move the volatility slider left to see the effect of volatility crush. After earnings, a drop of 20–40% is common.
  4. Touch or hover over the chart to read the profit or loss at any SPCX price.

The blue line is the value on your chosen date and the dashed line is the value at expiration. The shaded band is the typical move options are pricing in by expiration. If the breakeven sits outside that band, you need a bigger-than-usual move to make money.

Choose Spread to also sell a further-out option. It lowers the cost and caps the gain, which can make sense when options are expensive. All numbers are estimates from a standard pricing model; real prices will differ.

"Option chain heat map"

Every strike (rows) and expiration (columns) in one grid. Switch between Calls and Puts, and between Volume today (what traded today) and Open interest (contracts that already exist). Cells with stronger color have more contracts, which shows where traders are concentrating their bets. The outlined row is the strike nearest today's price. Tap any cell to load that contract into the calculator.

"Option prices over time"

The orange line is implied volatility (how expensive options are) and the gray line is how much SPCX actually moved. Dots mark earnings (E) and lockups (L). Implied volatility usually climbs before earnings and drops right after. The orange line started on Oct 21, 2026 and grows by one point each trading day.

"Upcoming events"

Earnings, lockup expirations (when insiders are allowed to sell shares), Fed meetings and monthly options expirations. Items marked (unconfirmed) are estimates, so check them before relying on the date.

"Options activity"

  • Put/call ratio: below 0.7 means traders are mostly betting up; above 1.0 means mostly betting down or buying protection.
  • Unusual activity: contracts trading far more than their existing open contracts. This can be a big trader making a bet. Contracts expiring today are usually just day-traders.

"Market context" and "Key price levels"

The Nasdaq-100, the VIX (the market's fear gauge; rising means nervous markets) and Tesla, along with how closely Tesla moves with SPCX. If the whole market is falling, a bullish SPCX signal is less reliable.

"Upcoming SpaceX launches" and "Latest headlines"

SpaceX's next launches, updated hourly. Starship test flights, Falcon Heavy and crewed missions are starred and also added to the events list, because big flights can move the stock. Launch dates slip often. Next to it are the latest SPCX headlines; tap one to read it on the publisher's site.

"SPCX vs. Tesla vs. Nasdaq-100"

Percentage change of all three over 1 month, 3 months or since the IPO. If SPCX moves with the Nasdaq-100, the move is probably about the whole market. If it moves alone, something SpaceX-specific is going on, so check the headlines and launches.

"Starship flight tracker"

Every recent Starship test flight with its result (success, failure or partial failure) and how SPCX moved afterward. Flights during market hours are measured against that day's close; flights after the close or on weekends are measured on the next trading day. The chips at the top compare the average move after successes and after failures. That's useful context before the next flight, which is shown when one is scheduled.

"Insider selling tracker"

After the IPO, most SpaceX shares were locked: employees, early investors and Elon Musk couldn't sell them until set dates. This section shows:

  • The next unlock: how many shares become eligible to sell and how much that grows the pool of tradable shares.
  • The bar: how much of the company is freely tradable now, how much unlocks by December 8, and how much (mostly Musk's) stays locked into 2027.
  • The table: every unlock date with its share count and how SPCX moved that day.

An unlock only means shares can be sold. So far SPCX has risen on most unlock days, so don't assume an unlock means a drop. The schedule lives in data/lockups.yaml and can be edited if SpaceX announces changes.

"Analyst price targets"

Where Wall Street analysts think SPCX will trade in about a year. The bar runs from the lowest to the highest target, with the average marked and a dot for today's price. Next to it are the consensus rating (1 = strong buy, 5 = sell), the mix of ratings this month, and recent upgrades and downgrades. Treat targets as opinions; they're often raised after the stock rises and cut after it falls.

"How SPCX moved around past events"

What the stock actually did after each earnings report and lockup date: the next-day move and the 3-day move. From now on, the app also records how big a move options were pricing in just before each event, so you can see whether options tend to overprice SPCX's events. If moves keep coming in smaller than priced in, buying options right before earnings has been a losing bet.

"How has this signal done?"

A test of the scoring rules on SPCX's past prices. Right direction is how often the stock moved the way the signal said. Around 50% is no better than a coin flip, and that's roughly where it stands today. Check this table occasionally. If a signal type stays near 50% after a few more months of data, give it little weight.

"Which signals have actually worked on SPCX?"

The score is built from about a dozen individual signals. This table tests each one on its own against SPCX's history:

  • Helpful: right at least 58% of the time, with a positive average move.
  • No edge: about a coin flip.
  • Misleading: wrong more often than right. For example, it said "drop" and the stock usually rose.
  • Too few: under 10 examples, so it can't be judged yet. These rows are hidden until you tap "Show more."

When the "Why" box lists a reason, check here whether that signal has been helpful or misleading on SPCX before giving it weight.

"Strong-signal history & results"

Every time the score crosses ±40, it's logged here with how SPCX moved 1, 5 and 10 trading days later. Green means the signal was right; red means it was wrong. Over time this is the most honest measure of whether the app is helping.

Weekly recap page

Open it from 📅 Weekly recap at the top. A bar chart shows each week's gain or loss, followed by a card per week: a short written recap, open and close, the range, best and worst day, volume compared with usual, events, any strong signals with how they turned out, and the daily-chart score at week's end. The current week is marked "In progress." It's a good Friday-evening read for spotting patterns.

3. A simple daily routine

1

Open the dashboard (once a day is enough)

It updates every hour during market hours. After the close is a calm time to look. Read "Today in one paragraph" and glance at the headlines. If the headline says "No clear signal — wait," you're done.

2

Check the main box and "Why"

Is the score past ±40? Are the top reasons from the daily chart, and do daily and hourly agree? Check "Which signals have actually worked" for the top reasons.

3

Check option prices

Look at "Are options cheap or expensive?" and the events list. Expensive options or an event in the next few days means caution.

4

Test a contract in the calculator

Tap Try it on a suggested contract. Is the breakeven inside the shaded typical-move band? What happens if SPCX doesn't move for two weeks? If earnings are coming, slide volatility down 30% to see the crush.

5

Decide, then trade at your broker

If you trade, use a limit order (set your own price) near the mid price rather than a market order. Decide in advance how much you're willing to lose.

Waiting is a valid choiceMost days won't have a strong signal. Buying options without a reason costs money every day through time decay.

4. Worked example

Here's how to read a real snapshot from October 21, 2026, the morning after SpaceX's Q3 earnings:

  • SPCX at $163.66, up 10.7% after earnings.
  • Score +24, "Leaning bullish — wait for confirmation." Daily +17, hourly −3.
  • Top reasons: price above its 20-day average, a daily uptrend, and a jump on 4.6× normal volume. Against those: hourly RSI 76 (overbought) and price above the upper hourly band (stretched).
  • Options fair: IV 58.5% versus 61.3% actual volatility.
  • Events: a lockup tranche the next day, with insiders allowed to sell more shares.

How to read it: The trend is up, but the stock just leapt 10% and the hourly chart says it's stretched. A lockup the next day could add selling pressure. The score is short of the ±40 strong-signal level. A reasonable reading is "positive, but don't chase the jump; wait to see if it holds after the lockup." If your friend already owned calls from before earnings, the dashboard's mix of a big gain, a stretched price and an upcoming lockup would make taking some profit worth considering.

5. Put it on your Home Screen

The dashboard can sit on your phone like a regular app, opening full-screen with its own icon.

  • iPhone: open the dashboard in Safari, tap the Share button, then Add to Home Screen. If you opened the link inside another app, first tap the compass icon to open it in Safari.
  • Android: open it in Chrome, tap the ⋮ menu, then Install app or Add to Home screen. The dashboard may also offer an Install button at the top.

It still updates hourly. If your phone is offline, it shows the last version it loaded.

6. Change events and settings

Both files can be edited on GitHub with the pencil icon. The dashboard updates a few minutes after you commit.

data/events.yaml

Add Starship launches, the next earnings date once SpaceX announces it, or anything else that could move the stock. Change verified: false to true once you've confirmed a date at ir.spacex.com.

config.yaml

  • signal_min_score: 40: raise it to 50 or 60 so only the strongest signals count; lower it for more.
  • min_days_to_expiry / max_days_to_expiry: how far out suggested contracts expire.

To refresh immediately instead of waiting for the hour: Actions → Update SPCX signals → Run workflow.

7. Common mistakes to avoid

  • Buying right before earnings. Volatility crush can wipe out gains even when you guess the direction right.
  • Buying very short-dated options. Contracts expiring this week are cheap because they usually expire worthless.
  • Ignoring the spread. A 15% gap between buy and sell prices means you start 15% behind.
  • Using market orders. Use limit orders near the mid price.
  • Holding into the last three weeks. Decay speeds up sharply, so sell or roll earlier.
  • Betting too much on one signal. With about a 50% hit rate so far, size each trade so a total loss is fine.
  • Treating the app as an oracle. It organizes the numbers; you still make the call.

8. Glossary

Bid / ask / mid
The bid is the highest price buyers offer, the ask is the lowest price sellers accept, and the mid is halfway between.
Breakeven
The stock price at expiration where you neither make nor lose money: strike plus the price paid for a call, strike minus the price paid for a put.
Call / Put
A call profits when the stock rises; a put profits when it falls.
Call spread / put spread
Buying one option and selling another further out. It costs less and limits both gains and losses. Useful when options are expensive.
Delta
How much the option's price moves for a $1 move in the stock. It also serves as a rough chance of finishing in the money.
Expiration
The last day the option exists. After that it's worth either its intrinsic value or nothing.
Implied volatility (IV)
The amount of future movement baked into option prices. Higher IV means pricier options.
IV rank
Today's IV compared with its own past range, from 0 to 100.
Lockup expiration
The date when insiders and employees become allowed to sell shares they held before the IPO. It can add selling pressure.
MACD
A momentum indicator. When it crosses up, momentum is turning positive; when it crosses down, negative.
Moving average
The average price over the last N periods, which smooths out noise to show the trend.
Open interest
How many contracts currently exist for that option. Higher means easier to trade.
RSI
A 0–100 momentum gauge. Above 70 is overbought; below 30 is oversold.
Strike
The price at which the option lets you buy (call) or sell (put) the shares.
Support / resistance
Price levels where the stock has repeatedly stopped falling (support) or rising (resistance).
Typical move
About how far SPCX could move by a date, based on option prices (one standard deviation). The stock stays inside it roughly two-thirds of the time.
Theta / time decay
The value an option loses each day just from time passing.
Black-Scholes
The standard formula for estimating an option's value from the stock price, strike, time left and volatility. The calculator uses it.
Volatility crush
The sharp drop in option prices right after a big expected event like earnings.
